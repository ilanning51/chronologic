# Flow as criteria for Chronologic

These are notes I collected on Csikszentmihalyi's conditions for flow, kept close to their original wording. The app exists for one thing: closing the gap between seeing what I need or want to do and actually going to do it. I have ADHD, so that gap is the whole problem, and every condition below is read as a criterion the app has to meet rather than a theory to admire.

Sources: https://siyli.org/resources/blog/flow-state, https://www.flowcentre.org/9-dimensions-to-flow, https://flowleadership.org/flow-conditions-csikszentmihalyis-summary/

## Core pre-conditions

Challenge-skill balance. The difficulty of the task must closely match your current abilities. If the task is too easy, you feel bored. If it is too hard, you feel anxious or stressed.

Criterion: a stretch is chosen to match the energy you have right now, which is why kinds are Deep, Shallow, Body and Out rather than projects, and a stretch never holds more than fits, as the line runs out at 80 percent of the declared time. The app cannot make a task harder or easier. However, it can keep you from loading a Deep stretch with twelve stations and calling it a plan.

Clear goals. You need a well-defined direction and immediate, moment-to-moment targets rather than vague objectives.

Criterion: at every zoom there is exactly one next station and the train points at it. A station is never bigger than a square, as a target you cannot start on is not a target. When a square is next and you cannot begin, the right move is to split it into circles, and the app should make that one gesture.

Immediate feedback. You must receive prompt information on how well you are performing so you can adjust your actions in real time.

Criterion: every completion changes the picture at once. The shape shrinks to a dot, the train slides on, a short tone sounds, and the pie is always a glance away. There is no end of day report, as the feedback has to arrive within the second rather than within the day.

## Key characteristics and mental conditions

Deep concentration. Your attention narrows completely onto the single task, shutting out outside stimuli and everyday distractions.

Criterion: the app never interrupts a running stretch. No notifications, no badges, no "you are behind", and nothing pinned to the screen beyond the four corner stickers. Zoomed in on the stretch, the line is the only thing on screen with any weight.

Action-awareness merging. Your body and mind sync up so completely that the action feels automatic and effortless, rather than forced.

Criterion: every core action is one gesture on the thing itself. Drawing is dragging through stations, completing is tapping the station, ending is holding the clock. There are no forms, no confirm dialogs and no menus on the way to doing the thing.

Sense of control. You possess a strong internal feeling of managing your own performance and actions without fear of failure.

Criterion: no state of the app is a failure state. Nothing is overdue, nothing is scored, a done station can be restored with a tap and a line can be redrawn at any moment. The plan is a route I own rather than a contract I signed.

Loss of self-consciousness. Your inner critic, ego, and self-doubt fade away as self-reflection shuts down temporarily.

Criterion: the app has no opinion of me. No streaks, no productivity statistics, no count of how many times a station slid to the front, no history in the first version. The inner critic needs numbers to work with, therefore the app shows none.

Transformation of time. Your subjective perception of time warps, making hours feel like minutes or minutes stretch out.

Criterion: time is shown only as a shrinking shape. The pie has no digits and the plane has no hours, which suits a brain that cannot feel time passing anyway, as a glance at the pie says "about a third left" without starting a calculation. When the pie empties the stretch ends on its own with one quiet sound, which is the only guard against hyperfocus the app will ever have.

Autotelic experience. The activity is intrinsically rewarding and performed for its own sake rather than just for an external reward.

Criterion: using the app should itself feel good. Drawing a line that snaps to 45 degrees, watching the color run out, hearing a station complete. This is the reason the look is borrowed from a game, as Mini Metro is played for its own sake and no planner ever has been.

## The friction rule

Everything above reduces to one measurement. The distance from opening the app to knowing the single next thing has to be zero gestures, therefore the app opens on the train at the stretch zoom whenever a stretch is running. The distance from having a thought to having a station has to be one tap and a few words, therefore capture is a line in the HUD and never a sheet. The distance from a plan to a started stretch has to be three gestures, a bullet, a chip and a drag.

If a future feature adds a gesture to any of those three distances, it is the wrong feature.
