# Chronologic

A planning tool for one person. It plans in loose chronological order, without timestamps, and keeps the thing you are planning for on the page next to the next step.

A project is one subway line, drawn from where you are to the goal you want. The stops along it are steps (a circle), sub-goals (a triangle) and the goal (a square, always last). Each is small, medium or large for its effort. The length of the line between two stops is the time you give yourself to get there, and no date or number appears anywhere.

A train rides each line in real time. It starts slowly, cruises, and slows into every stop, where it waits until you tap that stop. It never passes a stop on its own, and nothing is ever overdue, as the train just waits. Zoom in to watch the trains move. Bubbles at the screen edge point at each line's goal, so the reason for the next step is never out of sight.

Everything lives on one infinite plane. The look is borrowed from Mini Metro and from the Vignelli map of the New York subway.

## Using it

- Pan with one finger on empty paper. Zoom with two fingers, or with the mouse wheel.
- The squares on the paper are time. The legend, bottom left, says what one square is right now, and the unit changes as you zoom: 1 hour, 6 hours, 1 day, 1 week, 30 days, 3 months, 1 year, 5 years.
- Tap the plus, bottom right, to open or close the tray.
- Drag a step or a sub-goal out of the tray onto a line. It snaps to the line ahead of the train, and where you let go is where it splits the leg. Type a name and press Return.
- Tap New line in the tray, type the goal's name and press Return. Then drag the square to where the goal really is.
- Nothing goes on the life line alone: every square on it is a project's goal, moved there. To put a broad goal on it, such as a city to live in, make it a new line and drag its goal onto the life line. Deleting the project takes its square off the life line too. A marker saved before this rule stays where it was.
- Drag a stop anywhere to re-route the line through it. Only the two legs beside it change.
- Drag a stop and let go on another line to add it to that line too. It stays on its own line as well. Passing over a line on the way does nothing; only letting go counts.
- The life line is the one straight ink line, running across the whole paper at its own height, and it is always there. Only squares sit on it, so a step or a sub-goal dropped on it is refused and shown muted. Its train is the day line's kind: it reaches a goal when that goal's own train does, and rides real time only into a marker nothing feeds. The ink behind its train is muted.
- Drag a project's goal and let go on the life line, to the right of its train, to join it there. The goal takes the life line's height, the line before it stays as it was, and it is drawn as any goal is. A goal on the life line that a project works toward is called a ring here; a marker is a square nothing feeds, and only the project line running into a ring tells the two apart.
- Drag a ring or a marker along the life line to move it in time. Its height stays on the line, it never goes at or left of the life train, and only the legs beside it change, the last leg of a ring's project among them.
- Pull a ring well away from the life line, three times as far as a snap reaches, and it comes off: it is its project's goal again, and the drag goes on stretching its line. Let go back on the life line and it joins again. A marker has no other place, so it stays on the line, and while project lines are hidden zoomed out a ring stays on too. While held on the line a square leans a little toward your finger, so the hold shows, and springs back when you let go.
- A stop on two or more lines is drawn as a ring. It is one piece of work, so completing it completes it on every line.
- Drop a step or a sub-goal from the tray where two lines run close and it joins both.
- While you drag a stop or the goal, a chip beneath it shows how long the leg into it is, so you can set the length to your estimate. It goes when you let go. A tile carried onto a line shows the same for the leg it would end.
- Drag the goal to stretch or squeeze everything ahead of the train. The line behind the train does not move.
- Drag the start of a line to move where it begins. While the train is still on the first leg, the chip shows that leg's time; after that the start is history and moves freely.
- With a mouse, hold Shift and drag on empty paper to draw a box. Everything inside it, stops, dots and line starts, is picked up, and dragging inside the box moves it all together, nothing scaling. Tap on paper, tap the box, or press Escape to let it go.
- Tap the next stop to complete it, whether the train has arrived or not. Other stops ignore a tap.
- On the life line a tap on a marker completes it whenever it is the next square, whether its train has arrived or not. A tap on a ring belongs to its project, so it completes when the project's own next stop would.
- A done square on the life line is not a dot. It stays as a small ink square with its name, a landmark, and its name is kept when names are thinned. The left of the life line is the ones you have done.
- Long press a stop to edit its name, shape and size, or to delete it. Long press the most recent dot to bring it back with Not done.
- On a stop that sits on several lines, the editor also shows a chip in each line's colour. Tap a chip to take the stop off that line.
- A ring's editor shows a chip named Life, even when the ring is the last square on the life line. Tap it to take the goal off the life line; nothing is left behind and the project keeps its goal. A project's goal that is not on the life line, and stands right of its train, shows a hollow Life chip instead: tap it to put the goal on the life line where it stands, as dropping it there would. Deleting a project whose goal is a ring keeps that square on the life line as a marker.
- The tray has a sun. Drag it through the stops you mean to do today, in the order you mean to do them, and let go where the day should end. An ink line runs through them with its own train, which reaches each stop exactly when that stop's own train does. While today's day is drawn, the app opens on its train.
- Drag the small square at the end of the day through another stop to add that stop to the day. Drag it anywhere else to move it. Long press it and hold Delete to clear the day; the lines it ran through stay as they are.
- A day clears itself at midnight. Drawing a new day with the sun replaces today's.
- Tap the calendar button above the plus to open the week window over the map, and tap it again, press Escape or tap the map to close it. It opens on tomorrow. The row at the top is this week, Monday to Sunday: today is red and the chosen day is filled in ink. The arrows step a week at a time.
- The left of the window lists the next stop of each line, soonest first. The right is the chosen day from midnight at the top to midnight at the bottom, and it scrolls.
- Drag a next stop onto the day to plan it. Its block runs from where you let go for as long as its size suggests: 30 minutes for a small stop, an hour for a medium one, two hours for a large one. The block ends when the stop's train pulls in, so the stop slides along its line until its train arrives then. Each planned day is its own day line.
- Drag a block to move it, and the stop slides with it. Drag its bottom edge to make it longer or shorter; its start stays put. Drag it onto the list on the left to take it off the day; the stop stays where the block put it.
- A block snaps to the quarter hour. It cannot end before the stop before it on its line arrives, nor after the stop after it arrives. A drop that cannot happen is shown muted and changes nothing.
- Hold a dragged block over another day's button for half a second to turn to that day, and let go there to plan it on that day.
- On a touchscreen, rest a finger on a block or a next stop for a moment before dragging it. A finger that moves at once scrolls the day.
- Tomorrow's day line is drawn on the map faint and thin, as a sketch, with its train waiting at its start until tomorrow comes. Days further ahead live only in the week window.
- Hold the Delete button on a goal's editor to delete the whole line. Deleting a line and clearing the day are the only things in the app that ask you to confirm.
- Tap a bubble to change what it points at (next stop, next sub-goal, goal). Long press it to fly there.
- One bubble is filled with its line's colour: the line whose train reaches its next stop soonest. The others stay paper.
- A new line with only its goal is drawn faint and thin, as a sketch, until you give it a step.
- When a train has waited at a stop for a long time compared with the leg into it, a few small dots gather beside the stop. They go when you complete it.
- Tap a train to zoom in and watch it. Tap it again to go back.
- From far away the map draws only its big shapes, as a map draws cities and not streets. Zoom in and the smaller ones appear, in step with the legend: sub-goals come in at a week and a day, steps at six hours, an hour and fifteen minutes, larger first. The next stop and the goal are always drawn, and so is a stop you just added, until you next zoom.
- Zoom out further for the life line. From 3 months a square, project lines are not drawn, and only the life line and its squares remain. From 1 year, rings go too, and the squares left are markers and the ones done. The zoom reaches 5 years on a landscape phone.

## Running it

Install the dependencies once, then start the development server and open the address it prints.

```bash
npm install
```

```bash
npm run dev
```

The tests cover the model, the train's motion, the layout, the camera, the gesture recognizer, storage and the components, and they run without a browser.

```bash
npm test
```

A production build lands in `dist`, and `npm run preview` serves it so the install flow can be tried on a phone on the same network.

```bash
npm run build
```

The app ships a web app manifest, therefore it installs to the home screen from the browser's share or menu and opens in landscape. Everything is saved in the browser's local storage on the device it was made on, under the key `chronologic.v5`, as there is one user and one device at a time. On opening it reads v5, and failing that v4, then v3, then v2; the older keys are never written to, and the next save writes v5. A world read from before the life line gets an empty one below its lines. A v5 world that fails validation opens as an empty world, and its text is first copied to `chronologic.v5.refused`, once and never overwritten, so it can be recovered by hand. A world saved by the first version is left alone and ignored.
